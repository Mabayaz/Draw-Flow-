Lesson 02 / Perspective

Make space believable.

Perspective creates the illusion of depth by organizing lines, scale, and position around a horizon and vanishing points.

Perspective and space

In Solid Drawing, perspective makes a two-dimensional image feel three-dimensional. It helps a character move closer to the camera, an object recede, or a room hold together from one frame to the next.

Objectives

  • Identify the horizon line, vanishing point, and orthogonal lines.
  • Construct simple forms in one-point and two-point perspective.
  • Use distance and viewpoint to control size and proportion.

The horizon line

The horizon line represents the viewer’s eye level. Raising or lowering it changes whether we look down on a subject or look up at it.

One-point perspective

Use one vanishing point for a scene viewed straight on. Parallel depth edges travel toward that point, while verticals and horizontals remain organized around the picture plane.

Two-point perspective

Use two vanishing points when the object is turned. Left-facing and right-facing edges converge toward different points on the horizon.

Practice prompt

Draw a box three times: front-facing, turned slightly left, and turned slightly right. Keep the horizon fixed and compare how the edges change.
