Lesson 01 / Form

Give your drawings weight.

Form is the foundation of believable drawing. Learn to see the cube, sphere, cylinder, and cone inside the subject before adding detail.

Form and volume

Form describes the appearance of a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional surface. It has length, width, and depth. Shape is flat; form feels like something you could hold.

Objectives

  • Identify basic forms inside complex subjects.
  • Construct cubes, spheres, cylinders, and cones from different angles.
  • Use contour lines and overlap to communicate volume.

Start with primitives

A sphere gives you a round volume, a cube gives you planes and corners, and a cylinder gives you an axis and turning surface. Most characters, props, and environments can be simplified into combinations of these forms.

Turn the object

Draw each primitive from above, below, and three-quarter views. Add a centerline to the sphere and cylinder. Keep the construction lines light so you can check whether the volume stays consistent as it turns.

Practice prompt

Choose a household object. Draw it once as a collection of primitives, then draw it again with the construction hidden. Compare the two versions and note where the volume became unclear.

Key reminder

Detail cannot rescue a weak structure. Establish the big forms, their proportions, and their relationship to one another before polishing the surface.
