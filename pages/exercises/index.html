Form / Introduction

Make flat marks feel solid.

Form is the foundation of believable drawing. You will practice turning lines into cubes, spheres, cylinders, and combined objects by paying attention to volume, proportion, centerlines, and the way surfaces turn in space.

Each level has three moments: trace the construction, redraw it from memory, then compare your result with the original.

Stage one / unlocked

Form and volume

Turn flat marks into objects with weight. Start with the primitive forms that support almost every character, prop, and environment.

Ready to practice
Cube
Sphere
Cylinder

Exercise A: Primitive inventory

Draw a cube, sphere, and cylinder from three angles. Add a centerline to each one so the volume remains clear when it turns.

Exercise B: Combine the forms

Build a simple object from at least two primitives. Keep the overlaps visible and check that the parts share the same scale.

Complete both studies before moving to perspective.

Stage two / locked

Perspective and space

Use horizon lines and vanishing points to place your forms inside a believable world.

Locked
🔒 Complete Stage 1 to unlock this exercise.
One-point practice Draw a box facing you. Send every depth edge toward one vanishing point on the horizon.
Two-point practice Turn the box. Establish left and right vanishing points before drawing its top and bottom edges.
03

Stage three / locked

Shadow

Use a clear light source to describe core shadows, form shadows, and cast shadows without flattening the object.

Locked
🔒 Complete Stage 2 to unlock this exercise.
Core shadow

Find the turning point where the form faces away from the light.

Cast shadow

Project the object’s shadow across the receiving surface.

Light source

Keep highlight, half-tone, and shadow consistent with one direction.

Identify the light source before you shade.
